Setting Up Utilities

Internet. Electricity. Water.

01. Overview

Once your lease is signed and you have sorted out your initial furniture logistics, the final step to making your UK house functional is setting up your essential utilities. Navigating the British system involves dealing with regional monopolies, an open energy market, and entirely distinct telecom networks.

Here is exactly how to manage water, electricity, and the internet without any service interruptions.

02. Internet

Unlike the US, where internet providers are often strict regional monopolies, the UK internet market is highly competitive. Most providers use the same national Openreach fiber network, meaning you have choices.

Check the Speed First: Before picking a

provider, type your new UK postcode into the Uswitch Broadband Checker to see whether your specific street has access to standard copper broadband, superfast Fibre to the Cabinet (FTTC), or lightning-fast Ultrafast Full Fibre (FTTP). ● Where to Shop: Major reliable providers

include BT (British Telecom), Sky, and Virgin Media (Virgin operates on its own separate independent cable network and often boasts the highest speeds). ● The Trajectory Danger (Contract Length vs. [link] [link]

DERA): Most UK broadband contracts are locked in for 18 or 24 months. If your military tour is cut short or you move houses, breaking these contracts early triggers massive exit fees. Look into providers that offer shorter 12-month terms or flexible rolling options, or verify if the provider honors a "military clause" for forced relocation. ● Setup Timeline: Book your broadband setup

the day your lease is signed. It frequently takes two to three weeks for an engineer to activate your line or visit the property to install a new router.

03. Electricity

The UK electricity market is entirely deregulated, allowing you to choose your supplier. However, when you first move in, the property will already have a "default" supplier attached to it. Do not change your energy supplier without your landlord’s explicit permission.

On Moving Day: Find the physical electricity

meter in your house. Take a clear, timestamped photograph of the numbers on the meter screen. Call the existing utility provider immediately to open an account in your name starting from that exact baseline reading. This guarantees you are not charged for power used by previous tenants. ● Understanding the Energy Price Cap: The

UK energy market is regulated by a government body called Ofgem. Ofgem sets a strict Energy Price Cap every three months, which limits the maximum amount a supplier can charge you per kilowatt-hour (kWh) of electricity on a standard variable tariff. ● Fixed vs. Variable Tariffs: You can choose

to stay on a Standard Variable Tariff (prices fluctuate up or down with the market Cap) or lock into a Fixed-Rate Tariff (guarantees your per-unit price won't change for 12 months). Popular tech-forward suppliers like Octopus Energy are highly recommended within the expat community for their transparent billing and easy-to-use apps. ● Standing Charges: Be aware that your bill [link]

will include a daily Standing Charge (roughly 50p to 60p a day) just to keep your home hooked up to the local power grid,

even if you are away on leave and using zero electricity.

04.Water

Setting up water is the easiest utility process because you do not have to shop around. Water services are strictly location-dependent regional monopolies; you cannot change your supplier.

Finding Your Supplier: If you are stationed

around the Suffolk/Norfolk area (RAF Lakenheath, Mildenhall, or Feltwell), your regional water supplier will be Anglian Water. ● How to Register: Visit the Anglian Water [link] [link]

"Moving Home" Portal as soon as you move in. Enter your postcode, register as the new occupant, and submit your initial move-in date. ● Metered vs. Unmetered:

Metered: If your home has a water

meter, you will pay exactly for what you consume. Like electricity, locate the water meter on day one, take a photo of the dial, and submit the initial reading. ○ Unmetered: If the property does not

have a meter, you will pay a flat rate called a "Rateable Value" based on the size and estimated value of the property, regardless of how much water you use. Vital UK Utility "Pro-Tips" for Military Families

Council Tax Exemption (Crucial!): UK

residents pay a monthly local tax called

Council Tax. However, all US military personnel residing in the local community on official PCS orders are exempt from Council Tax. To claim this, obtain a completed exemption form and a copy of your orders, and submit them to your local district council. Check with your base housing office for assistance, as they hold the forms for most local councils. ● The TV License: In the UK, you must

purchase an annual TV License to watch live television broadcasts or use the BBC iPlayer streaming service. If you only watch subscription services like Netflix, Amazon Prime, or Disney+, you do not need one, but you must actively apply for a waiver at www.tvlicensing.co.uk. ● Renter's Insurance: While your landlord is [link]

legally responsible for insuring the building structure itself, they are not responsible for your personal belongings. Be sure to purchase renters insurance to cover your household items against fire and theft. ● Absentee Rule: If you plan to travel or be

away from your home for more than 7 consecutive days, always notify your landlord. During the winter, you must take precautions to guard against burst frozen pipes.
